Solar MPPT Battery Charger

A custom buck-converter charge controller that hunts the panel's maximum-power point in real time — harvesting roughly 30% more energy than the cheap PWM unit it replaced.

Off-grid setups in Kuwait's harsh sun lose a surprising amount of available solar energy to cheap PWM controllers that simply clamp the panel to battery voltage. I wanted to see how much of that loss I could recover with a proper switching converter and a tracking algorithm I controlled end to end.

Overview

The result is a 12 V / 10 A synchronous buck charge controller built around an STM32F103. A current-sense amplifier and resistor divider feed the panel's voltage and current into the ADC; firmware runs a perturb-and-observe MPPT loop at 50 kHz PWM to keep the panel sitting on its power knee as irradiance and temperature drift through the day.

Technical approach

I laid out a 2-layer PCB in KiCad with a tight power loop, a low-side current shunt, and gate-drive for the high- and low-side MOSFETs. The firmware samples panel V and I every PWM cycle, computes power, and nudges the duty cycle toward higher power — backing off when efficiency drops. A small OLED reports live panel power, battery state, and harvested watt-hours.

Topology
Synchronous buck, 50 kHz, 12 V / 10 A
Controller
STM32F103 · perturb-and-observe MPPT
Peak efficiency
94% at 80 W load
Energy gain
~30% vs. PWM controller (100 W panel, clear day)
Challenge

Early prototypes oscillated around the power point and the high-side MOSFET ran hot — the gate drive was marginal and the MPPT step size was too aggressive.

Solution

I added a bootstrap gate driver, shrank the power loop on the PCB, and made the MPPT step adaptive — large steps when far from the peak, fine steps near it. Switching losses dropped and tracking settled cleanly within two seconds of a cloud passing.

Outcome

Over a week of bench and rooftop logging, the controller recovered close to a third more daily energy than the PWM unit it replaced, held 94% peak efficiency, and tracked stably through fast irradiance swings. The project became my reference design for every power-electronics build since.
